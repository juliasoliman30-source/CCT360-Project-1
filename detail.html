Steam Client Redesign Case Study

A medium-fidelity prototype created in Figma for a redesigned Steam Client interface that addressed user pain points emerged from usability testing.

Problem

As a group, we analyzed the existing Steam Client interface and identified several user pain points based on our own experience and a brief search of the client.

From there, we created two different usability tests to gain a greater consensus on what main pain points are. These included card sorts, tree tests, and watching a user utilize the client.

Users were unable to identify certain labels, could not accurately sort them into their proper categories, and expressed annoyance for redundancies.

Solution

Through the testing, we discovered that Steam's Store page was largely cluttered, making it harder for people to easily find what they are looking for, outside of the search bar.

We also discovered that some of the terminology was not clear to new gamers, so we decided to include tool tips, descriptions, and more information for users

Thus, we created a responsive medium-fidelity prototype in Figma that addressed these concerns, and also added new features outside of the store page, such as making modifiable profile badges.

Results

  • Users were able to identify terms quicker and sort labels into their associated categories correctly
  • Users could browse through the store page better, with a desire to spend more time on it now
  • Users could enjoy better customization on their profile, making it more unique to their tastes
